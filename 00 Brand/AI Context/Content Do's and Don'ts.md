## Purpose

This document defines the concrete, enforceable rules that govern all Completeful content produced by humans or AI. These rules feed directly into automation prompts (n8n workflows, GPT-4o system messages, Notion content templates). Any AI tool generating Completeful content should treat this list as a hard constraint set, not a suggestion.

---

## Formatting Rules

### Always Do
- Write in short, declarative sentences for social content. One idea per sentence.
- Use the active voice in every CTA. "Start your store," not "A store can be started."
- Capitalize product names and platform names correctly: Etsy (not etsy), TikTok Shop (not Tiktok shop), Shopify (not shopify), WooCommerce.
- Use numerals for statistics and quantities in social copy: "500+ products," "0 order minimums," "1 dashboard."
- Spell out numbers one through nine in editorial/blog contexts; use numerals from 10 up.
- Write US manufacturing as a specific, emphasized point, not a throwaway. It is a differentiator.
- End every piece of content with a single, unambiguous CTA.
- Carousel slides: each slide must function as a standalone shareable statement.
- For reel/video scripts, front-load the hook within the first two seconds of the script.

### Never Do
- Use emojis anywhere in finalized social copy, captions, ad copy, or marketing text.
- Use em dashes or en dashes as punctuation in any marketing-facing copy. Rewrite the sentence.
- Use ellipses (...) to create dramatic pauses in social copy. Use a new sentence or a period.
- Use filler openers: "In today's world," "Have you ever wondered," "As a seller, you know that..." These are weak and the audience will scroll past.
- Stack multiple CTAs in a single piece of content. One CTA per asset.
- Use the word "just" in a minimizing sense ("It's just a simple tool"). It undermines the value proposition.
- Use "we" language that centers Completeful over the seller. Reframe toward seller benefit.
- Write vague superlatives without evidence: "best," "fastest," "most reliable" need context or caveats.

## Claims: What to Assert vs What to Avoid

### Approved Claims
- No order minimums
- US-based, in-house manufacturing in Lafayette, Louisiana
- 500 to 900+ product catalog
- Direct integrations with Shopify, Etsy, TikTok Shop, and WooCommerce
- Transparent, publicly available pricing
- Fast domestic fulfillment (do not specify exact day counts without a current verified SLA on hand)
- High-quality print on demand
- 228,000 sq ft production facility
- Products made in the USA

### Claims to Avoid
- Specific fulfillment time guarantees (e.g., "ships in 2 days") unless pulled from a current, verified source
- Income or revenue projections for sellers
- Comparative claims against named competitors (e.g., "better than Printful") without published data
- Platform algorithm outcome claims ("This will get you more views on TikTok")
- Claiming exclusivity on products without verification ("only place you can get this")

## CTA Style Rules

Every piece of content ends with one CTA. CTAs at Completeful follow this structure:

- **Action verb + specific next step + implied benefit**

Good examples:
- "Start your store at completeful.com"
- "Connect your Etsy shop today"
- "See the full catalog at completeful.com"
- "Follow for more seller tips"

Poor examples (do not use):
- "Click the link in bio to learn more" (too vague, no benefit implied)
- "Check us out!" (casual to the point of being meaningless)
- "Subscribe and like for more content" (platform-mechanic ask with no seller benefit)

For carousel slide 7 (CTA slide), the CTA should name a specific action and the brand URL or handle.

## Platform-Specific Rules

### Instagram (feed carousels, reels)
- No emojis in caption copy
- Hashtags are allowed but treated as discoverability metadata, not part of the visible copy. Place at the end of the caption or in a comment.
- Caption tone: slightly warmer and more conversational than LinkedIn, but still no em dashes

### TikTok
- Scripts should sound spoken, not read. Write how the presenter actually talks.
- Hook must be in the first sentence of the script, treated as a hard rule.
- On-screen text should be minimal: maximum two lines visible at once.

### LinkedIn
- Slightly more formal than Instagram. Full sentences, no slang.
- Opening line must be a strong, standalone statement. LinkedIn truncates after roughly 210 characters before the "see more" break; the hook must work within that window.
- No emojis, no dashes, same as all platforms.

### YouTube
- Long-form scripts follow a hook (first 30 seconds) plus value delivery plus CTA structure.
- Titles follow the format: specific benefit or insight + context. Example: "How Etsy Sellers Scale With No Inventory Using US Print On Demand"
- Thumbnail text: two to four words maximum, Bebas Neue, high contrast.

## Banned Punctuation in Social Copy

The following punctuation patterns are banned from any finalized social-facing marketing copy at Completeful:

| Banned | Reason | Substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Em dash (--) | Off-brand, creates fragmented run-on feel | Period, new sentence, or comma |
| En dash (-) used as a separator | Same as above | Period or rewrite |
| Ellipsis (...) for dramatic pause | Feels dated, low-energy | Period and new sentence |
| Exclamation point overuse (more than one per piece) | Dilutes emphasis | One maximum, on the most important statement |
| All-caps entire sentences | Feels like shouting, not coaching | Bebas Neue handles visual emphasis; use title case at most |

These rules apply to copy generated by AI and humans alike. Any content review step in the automation pipeline should flag violations of these rules before publishing.
