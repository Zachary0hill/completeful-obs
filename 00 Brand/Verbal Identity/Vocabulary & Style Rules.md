## Overview

Consistent language is a form of quality control. When Completeful uses the same terms the same way across every channel, the brand feels coherent and trustworthy. When terminology drifts, the brand feels assembled from different voices. This document is the reference for every writer, editor, or AI system producing content under the Completeful brand.

---

## Capitalization

**Completeful** is always capitalized as a proper noun. Never "completeful" in running text. Never all-caps "COMPLETEFUL" unless it is a deliberate design treatment in a visual asset.

**Print-on-demand** is hyphenated when used as a compound modifier before a noun ("a print-on-demand platform") and as a standalone noun ("print-on-demand as a business model"). POD is the acceptable abbreviation in internal documents and after the full term has been introduced. Do not use POD without first establishing it in formal external content.

**3PL** is always written as an initialism, uppercase. Do not write "third party logistics" without hyphens; the correct form is "third-party logistics" when spelled out.

**DTG** is the abbreviation for direct-to-garment and is acceptable after introduction. Spell out on first reference in external-facing content.

**Etsy, Shopify, WooCommerce, TikTok Shop** are proper nouns and should be capitalized exactly as the platforms themselves capitalize them.

**US** (no periods). Not "U.S." Completeful's style is "US-made," "US-based," "US manufacturing."

**Lafayette** always followed by "Louisiana" on first reference in external content. Internal documents can use Lafayette alone.

## Words to Use

**Sellers** not "customers" when referring to the businesses and individuals who use Completeful to fulfill their own stores. Completeful serves sellers. Sellers serve their own customers. Maintaining this distinction keeps the language accurate and reinforces Completeful's role as a B2B partner.

**In-house** when referring to Completeful's production model. "In-house manufacturing," "made in-house," "produced in-house." This is the differentiating term and should appear consistently wherever the production model is discussed.

**Fulfillment** not "shipping" as the broader umbrella term when describing what Completeful does. Shipping is one step in fulfillment. Fulfillment includes receiving the order, producing the product, picking, packing, and shipping.

**Platform integrations** when referring to Shopify, Etsy, TikTok Shop, and WooCommerce connections. Not "plugins" or "apps" generically, as these have specific meanings within each platform ecosystem.

**Personalization** when referring to the category of products or services that add individual buyer-specific text, imagery, or customization. Personalization is the seller-facing term. The production methods that enable it (engraving, embroidery, laser) should be named specifically when relevant.

**Catalog** when referring to the full range of available products. "Product catalog" is fine on first reference; "catalog" alone works in subsequent references.

**Margin** not "profit" when discussing what sellers earn after costs. Sellers manage margin. Completeful helps them protect and improve it by offering competitive pricing.

---

## Words to Avoid

**Partner** when used loosely. "Our partner" can mean too many things. If Completeful is serving a seller, call them a seller. If Completeful is working with a platform integration, call it a platform integration. Reserve "partner" for formal business relationships where the word is accurate and specific.

**Cheap** in any context. Completeful's pricing is competitive and transparent. Cheap implies low quality. Use "competitive pricing," "transparent pricing," "affordable," or reference specific price points.

**Just** as a minimizer in sales or support contexts. "Just sign up," "it is just that easy" undermines the intelligence of the seller. Remove "just" and the sentence is stronger.

**Revolutionary**, **game-changing**, **disrupting** unless the context truly warrants it. These are category-level dilution terms. Completeful's claims are specific and grounded, not hype.

**We pride ourselves on** is a filler phrase. Instead of "we pride ourselves on fast turnaround," write "our turnaround is fast because production and shipping happen in the same facility."

**And more** at the end of a list when the list can be completed or the additional items are important. "And more" is an editorial shrug. If it matters, name it. If it does not matter enough to name, cut the phrase.

## Grammar and Style Conventions

**Oxford comma.** Always use the serial comma in lists of three or more items. "Engraving, embroidery, and laser printing" not "engraving, embroidery and laser printing."

**Active voice preferred.** "Completeful ships your orders" not "your orders are shipped by Completeful." Active constructions are faster and more confident.

**Numbers.** Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above. Exception: always use numerals for percentages (7%), measurements (228,000 sq ft), and product counts (500-plus products). "500-plus" with a hyphen when used as a modifier ("our 500-plus product catalog"); "500 or more" when used predicatively.

**Hyphens in compound modifiers.** When two or more words act as a single adjective before a noun, hyphenate: "print-on-demand platform," "US-made products," "in-house production," "seller-first approach."

**Sentence length.** Vary it. Long sentences can carry complex ideas. Short sentences land emphasis. A string of identical sentence lengths is monotonous regardless of content.

## Platform-Specific Notes

**Instagram (@completeful):** No emojis in finalized captions. No em dashes or en dashes. Short, declarative sentences. Hook in the first line, CTA in the last. Hashtags in a separate comment or at the end if required, never embedded in the caption text.

**TikTok (@completeful):** Caption should support the video, not duplicate it. Write the benefit, not the description of what is happening on screen. Same no-emoji, no-dash rules apply to the caption. The spoken script in a TikTok can use a slightly warmer, conversational register than the written caption.

**X (@completefultech):** Short. Single idea per post. If a thread is needed, the first post must stand alone as a complete thought. No emoji. No dash.

**LinkedIn (/company/completeful):** Slightly more formal register. The seller audience here includes brand owners and operations leaders evaluating vendors. Lead with a specific claim or data point, not a question. Long-form posts are appropriate but should earn their length with substance.

**YouTube (@Completeful):** Titles use title case. Descriptions should open with a plain-English summary of the video content, followed by relevant links (website, app, related content), followed by platform handles. No keyword stuffing.

**Blog (completeful.com/blog):** Follow AP style as a general baseline. Use headings to break content into scannable sections. Each post should answer one specific question a seller would actually search for. Avoid posts that try to cover too much ground.

## Quick Vocabulary Reference

| Use this | Not this |
|---|---|
| Sellers | Customers (when referring to Completeful's users) |
| In-house manufacturing | Third-party production / partner network |
| US-made | American-made (acceptable but US-made is preferred) |
| Print-on-demand (POD) | Print on demand (no hyphens), PoD |
| 3PL | 3pl, third party logistics, Third Party Logistics |
| Transparent pricing | Honest pricing, competitive pricing (use transparent specifically) |
| No order minimums | Zero minimums, no minimum orders |
| Completeful app | The app, our app (after first reference is fine) |
| Catalog | Catalogue (US spelling standard) |
| Fulfillment | Fulfilment (US spelling standard) |
