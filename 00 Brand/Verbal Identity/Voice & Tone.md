## The Completeful Voice

Completeful's voice is consistent across every channel and content format. It does not change from platform to platform. What changes is the tone, which is the specific calibration of that voice for a given context and audience state. Understanding the difference between voice and tone is what keeps Completeful's communications feeling human rather than scripted.

### Voice Attributes

**Coaching, not lecturing.** Completeful has real expertise in production, fulfillment, and seller economics. The voice delivers that expertise in a way that equips the reader to act, not in a way that establishes authority over them. The test: does this communication leave the seller more capable than before they read it? If yes, it is coaching. If it just displays knowledge, it is lecturing.

**Empowering, not hyping.** There is a difference between making a seller feel capable and making them feel excited. Hype wears off. Empowerment compounds. Completeful's content should give sellers tools, context, and confidence they can carry into their next decision, not just a momentary spike of enthusiasm.

**Expert but approachable.** Completeful operates six fiber lasers, sixteen embroidery machines, and twenty-eight engraving machines. It has the receipts to speak with authority. But authority in the voice should read as confidence, not as gatekeeping. The tone is collegial. We know this, and we want you to know it too.

**Seller-first.** Every communication should pass the test: who does this serve? If the answer is "it promotes Completeful," that is not sufficient. If the answer is "it helps a seller do something better," that is the target. Completeful earns seller trust by demonstrating it prioritizes seller outcomes, and the voice is the daily proof of that.

**Specific over vague.** Completeful does not say "fast shipping." It says "domestically produced and shipped from Lafayette, Louisiana." It does not say "a wide range of products." It says "500 to 900-plus products." Specificity is credibility. Vague claims are the sound of a brand that does not want to be held accountable.

## How Tone Shifts by Context

### Educational Content (Blog, YouTube, How-To)

The goal is to transfer knowledge and build seller capability. Tone here is patient, thorough, and structured. Assume the reader is smart but may not yet have domain knowledge. Break down mechanics. Define terms the first time they appear. Do not rush to the point at the expense of the reader actually understanding the point.

Appropriate tone markers: clear structure, step-by-step logic, occasional acknowledgment of seller frustration points ("this is where most new sellers get stuck"), specific examples.

**Example (educational):**
"Pricing for margin on a personalized item is different from pricing a standard print. Your base cost includes the product, personalization, and fulfillment. Before setting a listing price, add those three numbers and then work backward from the margin you need to stay profitable after platform fees."

### Promotional Content (Ads, Product Announcements, Platform CTAs)

The goal is to move a seller toward a specific action. Tone here is confident, direct, and benefit-forward. The seller should feel the pull of a clear offer. This is not the place for nuance or qualification. State the benefit, state the differentiator, state the action.

Appropriate tone markers: short sentences, active verbs, direct benefit statements, minimal subordinate clauses.

**Example (promotional):**
"Your design, our production. No minimums. Made in the USA. Start selling at completeful.com."

### Support and Customer-Facing Communications

The goal is to resolve a problem and restore confidence. Tone here is calm, direct, and accountable. Never defensive, never deflecting, never passive. Acknowledge the issue, explain what is happening, and tell the seller what happens next. If something went wrong, own it.

Appropriate tone markers: first-person accountability ("we"), specific next steps with timelines, no corporate hedging language.

**Example (support):**
"We see the issue on your order and we are correcting it now. You will receive a new tracking number within 24 hours. We are sorry for the delay."

## The No-Emoji, No-Dash Rule for Finalized Social Copy

For finalized social copy intended for publication, two rules apply without exception:

**No emojis.** Emojis are not part of Completeful's finalized social voice. They are a drafting aid and a brainstorming tool, but they do not appear in published captions, ad copy, or platform posts. The brand communicates with words.

**No em dashes or en dashes.** Dashes create a visual pause that works well in long-form writing but feels cluttered in short social copy. Write around them. A sentence that needs an em dash can almost always be restructured into two sentences or a comma construction that reads more cleanly.

These rules apply to: Instagram captions, TikTok captions, X posts, LinkedIn posts, YouTube descriptions, and any caption or short-form text that goes out under the Completeful brand.

They do not apply to: internal documentation, content briefs, draft copy, Notion notes, or this brand bible.

### Before and After Examples

**Before (violates rules):**
"Your products deserve better production quality than you are currently getting from overseas suppliers. Fast, US-made fulfillment is just a click away. Start now and see the difference."

**After:**
"Your products deserve better production quality than you are currently getting from overseas suppliers. Fast, US-made fulfillment is one click away. Start now and see the difference."

(The above example actually already complies. Here is a genuine violation corrected:)

**Before (violates rules):**
"In-house production, no minimums, 500+ products... everything you need to scale. No waiting on overseas orders."

**After:**
"In-house production, no minimums, 500-plus products. Everything you need to scale. No waiting on overseas orders."

## Quick Reference

| Context | Tone | Sentence length | Key attributes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Educational | Patient, thorough | Longer, structured | Knowledge transfer, seller capability |
| Promotional | Confident, direct | Short, punchy | Benefit-forward, clear CTA |
| Support | Calm, accountable | Direct | Acknowledgment, next steps, no hedging |
| Social copy | Expert, approachable | Short to medium | No emojis, no dashes, seller-first |
