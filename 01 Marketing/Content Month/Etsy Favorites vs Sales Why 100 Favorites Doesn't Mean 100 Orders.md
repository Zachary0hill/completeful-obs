**HOOK**
*Your listing has 500 favorites and 3 sales. Sound familiar? Here's why favorites don't equal revenue and what to fix.*

**KEY POINTS**
1. Favorites = interest, not intent. Most favorited items are saved for "someday"
2. High favorites + low sales usually means your price is too high for impulse buyers
3. Or your product looks great but lacks urgency (add "limited design" or seasonal messaging)
4. Conversion fix: add more listing photos, better descriptions, and social proof
5. Best metric to track: conversion rate (views ÷ purchases), not favorites

**CALL TO ACTION**
Fix the conversion, not the favorites count, sell quality products with Completeful

**60-SECOND REEL SCRIPT**
(0:00–0:03) HOOK: Five hundred favorites and three sales? Sound familiar? Here's why favorites don't pay your bills.
(0:03–0:15) Favorites feel like winning, but they're interest, not intent. Most people favorite something for "someday," and someday never comes. So stop celebrating that number.
(0:15–0:45) If you've got high favorites and low sales, two things are usually going on. One: your price is too high for an impulse buy, so people save it instead of buying. Two: your listing looks great but has zero urgency, no reason to act today. Fix it by adding "limited design" or seasonal messaging, stacking more listing photos, writing a stronger description, and showing social proof through reviews. Then stop tracking favorites entirely and track conversion rate, views divided by purchases. That's the number that tells the truth.
(0:45–0:55) Sellers who obsess over favorites stay broke. Sellers who fix conversion turn the same traffic into real orders.
