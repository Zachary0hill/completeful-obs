**LISTICLE:**

**1. Canva Free, great starting point, terrible finish line**
Canva gets you up and running fast and that's the problem. Everyone uses the same templates, same fonts, same elements. Your designs end up looking like a hundred other listings. It works for mockups and social content but if it's your only design tool, you're blending in.

**2. Canva Pro, worth it if you're serious**
Background remover, brand kit, premium elements, and way more fonts. For $13 a month it removes a lot of the limitations that make free Canva designs look generic. If you're not ready for advanced software this is the best middle ground.

**3. Photopea, the free Photoshop nobody talks about**
It runs in your browser, handles PSD files, and works almost exactly like Photoshop. Zero cost. If you want more control over your designs without paying for Adobe, this is the move most beginners don't know about.

**4. Adobe Illustrator, the industry standard for a reason**
Vector-based designs that scale to any product without losing quality. If you're building a real brand and want designs that look professional on everything from stickers to all-over-print hoodies, this is where the top sellers eventually land.

**5. The truth nobody tells you**
The tool doesn't make the designer. A great concept in Canva will outsell a mediocre design made in Illustrator every single time. Start with what you can afford, learn the fundamentals, and upgrade your tools as your revenue grows. Don't let software be the excuse you never start.
