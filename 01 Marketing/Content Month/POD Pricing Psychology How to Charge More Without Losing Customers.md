**HOOK**
*You're not charging too much, you're not communicating enough value. Here's the psychology behind premium pricing.*

**KEY POINTS**
1. Anchor pricing: show a "compare at" price to make your price feel like a deal
2. Use odd pricing ($27.99 feels significantly cheaper than $28.00)
3. Bundle products to shift perception from unit price to value
4. Lifestyle mockups increase perceived value by 30% vs white background
5. "Free shipping" built into price converts better than lower price + shipping fee

**CALL TO ACTION**
Price for profit, sell with confidence, Completeful's low costs give you pricing flexibility

**60-SECOND REEL SCRIPT**
(0:00–0:03) HOOK: You're not charging too much, you're just not communicating enough value. Here's the pricing psychology that lets you charge more.
(0:03–0:15) Sellers panic and drop prices the second something doesn't sell. But cheap doesn't convert, perceived value does. Fix the perception, not the number.
(0:15–0:45) Five moves. One: anchor it, show a "compare at" price so your real price feels like a deal. Two: use odd pricing, $27.99 reads way cheaper than $28 even though it's a penny. Three: bundle, because once it's a set, buyers stop comparing unit prices. Four: swap white-background photos for lifestyle mockups, that alone can lift perceived value around 30%. Five: build shipping into the price and label it free; "free shipping" beats a lower price plus a shipping fee almost every time.
(0:45–0:55) Stack these and you raise prices while customers feel like they're getting more, not less.
(0:55–0:60) CTA: Price for profit and sell with confidence, Completeful's low base costs give you the room to do it.
