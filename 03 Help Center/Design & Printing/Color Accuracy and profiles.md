# Color Accuracy and Profiles

Color accuracy is one of the most common concerns for print-on-demand sellers. Understanding how color works across different screens and print methods will help you set accurate expectations and deliver results your customers love.

## Why Colors Look Different on Screen vs. Print

Your monitor displays color using light (RGB — red, green, blue). Printers apply physical pigments or dyes to a surface. These are fundamentally different systems, and a color that looks vivid on your screen may print slightly differently depending on the product and print method.

Additionally, monitor calibration varies widely. A color that looks one way on your laptop may look different on another screen — neither is necessarily correct. Designing with a calibrated monitor and reviewing printed samples is the most reliable way to dial in your colors.

## Color Profiles Used by Completeful

All artwork should be submitted in **RGB** color mode. Completeful's production systems handle the conversion to the appropriate output profile for each print method.

- **DTG (direct-to-garment):** Uses pigment inks. Colors are generally accurate but can appear slightly less saturated on dark garments due to an underbase white layer. Use the garment color preview in the product builder to check contrast.
- **Sublimation (mugs, totes, puzzles):** Produces very vibrant, saturated colors because the dye bonds directly into the substrate. Colors often come out richer than they appear on screen. Avoid very pale or pastel designs if you want impact.
- **UV printing:** Accurate color reproduction on hard surfaces. Works well for both bold graphics and fine details.
- **Laser engraving:** Single-tone output (the engraved material color vs. the base material). Color artwork is interpreted as grayscale depth — lighter areas engrave less, darker areas engrave more.

## How to Improve Color Accuracy

1. **Calibrate your monitor** if you have color-critical work. Many operating systems include a built-in color calibration tool.
2. **Use the sRGB color space** as your working color space in design tools like Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop, Canva, or Procreate. sRGB is the standard for digital output and is what Completeful's systems expect.
3. **Order a physical sample** before listing products for sale. This is the only way to see exactly how your design prints on a specific product. Sample orders are available at cost.
4. **Avoid relying on hex or RGB values alone.** "Bright red" in RGB can shift subtly depending on the substrate. If color matching is critical (for example, a brand logo), order samples and adjust accordingly.
5. **Dark garments need attention.** DTG prints on black or dark-colored shirts use a white underbase that is applied first. This can slightly change how colors sit on the garment. Use the product builder's dark garment preview.

## What Completeful Cannot Guarantee

- Exact color matches to a Pantone swatch without physical color profiling (not part of standard production).
- Identical colors across different product types (a mug and a t-shirt using the same design will look different because the print method and substrate differ).
- Screen-accurate reproduction without a calibrated monitor on your end.

## Getting Help with Colors

If a production issue results in colors that look dramatically different from your approved design, that may qualify as a quality issue. See **Handling Quality Issues** for the process to report and resolve production problems. For color questions before you order, contact support@completeful.com.
