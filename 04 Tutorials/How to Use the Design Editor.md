**\[Chapter 2 | 2:00\] Uploading artwork the right way**

Setup:
A good editor can't fix a weak file. If your image is low resolution, cropped wrong, or built with the wrong background, the final product and the mockup will both suffer.

Tension:
Click upload and bring in your design file. \[Screen Recording: Uploading a PNG or artwork file into the Design Editor\] \[Text Overlay: Step 2: Upload artwork\]
For most beginner designs, a high-resolution PNG with a transparent background is the safest starting point because it keeps the artwork clean on the product preview. \[B-roll: Side-by-side example of transparent PNG versus white background file\] \[Text Overlay: Use high-resolution transparent files\]
After upload, check how the file appears on the canvas. If it looks soft, pixelated, or jagged right away, stop and replace the file before moving on. \[Screen Recording: Zooming in on an uploaded design to inspect detail\] \[Pattern Interrupt: Freeze frame with "Do not ignore this" text\] \[Text Overlay: Check file quality early\]
This is also the moment to compare your file to Completeful's artwork upload guidelines, especially if you're working with detailed text, thin lines, or small logos. \[Screen Recording: Opening artwork requirements/help reference in another tab, then returning to the editor\] \[Text Overlay: Follow artwork upload guidelines\]

Payoff:
When your file starts clean, every step after this gets easier. Better uploads mean better placement, better previews, and fewer surprises after the order is placed.

Transition sentence:
With your artwork uploaded, the next skill is the one that separates a rough mockup from a product people actually want to buy.

**\[Chapter 3 | 4:10\] Sizing and positioning your design**

Setup:
This is where most beginners either make the design too small out of fear or way too large because it looks good on a laptop screen. The editor helps, but you still need a simple process.

Tension:
Click your artwork layer and use the resize controls to scale it within the printable area. \[Screen Recording: Selecting design and dragging corners to resize\] \[Text Overlay: Step 3: Resize inside the safe area\]
Keep the design large enough to feel intentional, but never let important elements crowd the edge of the print zone. \[Screen Recording: Moving design too close to edge, then correcting it\] \[Text Overlay: Avoid edge crowding\]
Use alignment tools or visual centering to position the artwork cleanly. For chest prints on apparel, center horizontally and check the vertical placement twice before you save. \[Screen Recording: Centering artwork on front of shirt\] \[Text Overlay: Center first, then fine-tune\]
If the product supports multiple design areas, such as front and back or different customizable surfaces, switch between them and repeat the same check for each side. \[Screen Recording: Toggling between design areas/views\] \[Pattern Interrupt: Quick split-screen of front view and alternate view\] \[Text Overlay: Review every printable area\]
Zoom out after every adjustment. A design can look perfect at 200 percent and completely off at normal size. \[Screen Recording: Zooming in and out to compare placement\] \[Text Overlay: Zoom out before approving\]

Payoff:
At this stage, your design should look balanced, readable, and product-ready. You are no longer just placing art. You are building a listing customers can trust.

Transition sentence:
Once placement looks good in the editor, the next job is to make sure the product preview sells the idea clearly.

**\[Chapter 4 | 6:30\] Previewing and generating mockups**

Setup:
Your mockup is often the first thing shoppers see. If your preview is unclear or inconsistent, even a strong design can get ignored.

Tension:
Use the product preview inside the editor to inspect your design at a realistic size. \[Screen Recording: Rotating through product preview images\] \[Text Overlay: Step 4: Review the preview\]

Then generate mockups for your listing. \[Screen Recording: Clicking into mockup generation/export area\] \[Text Overlay: Generate mockups\]
Choose the cleanest product images that show the design clearly and match the style of store you're building, whether that's Shopify, Etsy, TikTok Shop, or WooCommerce. \[Screen Recording: Selecting mockup views and downloading or preparing assets\] \[Pattern Interrupt: Platform logos appearing briefly as examples\] \[Text Overlay: Works with Shopify, Etsy, TikTok Shop, WooCommerce\]

Payoff:
Now you have the visual assets you need to present the product professionally, and that makes the rest of your store setup much easier.

Transition sentence:
The final workflow step is saving your work correctly and making sure the product is ready to move forward.

**\[Chapter 5 | 8:20\] Saving, reviewing, and preparing for your store**

Setup:
A lot of beginner sellers forget to save their work clearly or skip the final review before publishing. That leads to version confusion and preventable mistakes.

Tension:
After your design is placed and your mockups are generated, save the product with a clear name that tells you exactly what it is. \[Screen Recording: Saving product and entering product name\] \[Text Overlay: Step 5: Save and name clearly\]

## BONUS

**\[Chapter 6 | 10:10\] The one placement check beginners should always do**

Earlier I mentioned I'd share the simplest placement check that can save your product page. Here it is: do the thumbnail test.

Open your product preview or generated mockup and shrink it until it is about the size a shopper would first see on a collection page, Etsy search result, or TikTok Shop grid. \[Screen Recording: Reducing mockup size on screen to thumbnail scale\] \[Text Overlay: Bonus: The thumbnail test\]
Then ask three questions. Can I read it? Can I tell what the product is? Does the design still feel centered and intentional? \[B-roll: Thumbnail-sized examples with good and bad placements\] \[Text Overlay: Readable? Clear? Balanced?\]
If the answer to any of those is no, go back into the Design Editor and adjust size, contrast, or placement before publishing. \[Screen Recording: Returning to editor and making a small placement correction\] \[Text Overlay: Fix it before you publish\]

This works because customers usually do not meet your product in full-screen first. They meet it as a tiny image in a crowded feed. If your design communicates well there, your listing starts much stronger.
