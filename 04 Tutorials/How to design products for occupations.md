## BODY OUTLINE

**\[Chapter 1: 0:00 Choosing the right occupation niche\]**

\[Setup\]
\[Spoken\] The biggest mistake beginners make is designing for an occupation that's too broad. "Teacher" or "mechanic" can work, but broad niches are more competitive and harder to make personal.
\[Text Overlay\] Broad sells slower. Specific connects faster.
\[Visual Cue\] Split screen showing generic "Best Teacher Ever" versus more specific occupation-themed product concepts

\[Tension\]
\[Spoken\] Inside your planning process, start by narrowing the audience one level deeper. Instead of "teacher," think math teacher, kindergarten teacher, band director, or special education teacher. Instead of "medical," think ER nurse, dental hygienist, physical therapist, or radiology tech.
\[Screen Recording\] Notes app or simple worksheet on screen with a list of occupations being narrowed into sub-niches
\[Text Overlay\] Step 1: Go one level deeper

\[Spoken\] Then ask three quick questions: what does this person do every day, what language do they use, and who usually buys gifts for them. That last question matters because occupation products are often purchased by spouses, coworkers, students, friends, and family.
\[Screen Recording\] On-screen checklist appears beside niche ideas
\[Text Overlay\] Daily role • insider language • gift buyer

\[Pattern Interrupt\]
\[Visual Cue\] Quick punch-in zoom with sound cue on the words "gift buyer"

\[Spoken\] Let's say we choose dental hygienist. That gives us clearer language, stronger humor, and better gift angles than a generic "healthcare worker" design.
\[Screen Recording\] Cursor highlights "Dental Hygienist" as the selected niche in planning notes
\[Text Overlay\] Example niche: Dental Hygienist

\[Payoff\]
\[Spoken\] At this stage, you've already made the rest of the process easier. Your product choices, wording, and visuals will all be more targeted, which is what helps your listing stand out.
\[Text Overlay\] Specific niche = easier product decisions

\[Transition\]
\[Spoken\] Now that the niche is clear, the next step is matching that audience with products they're actually likely to buy.

**\[Chapter 2: 2:10 Picking the best products for that occupation\]**

\[Setup\]
\[Spoken\] Not every occupation design belongs on every product. A strong idea can still underperform if it's placed on the wrong item.
\[Text Overlay\] Great design, wrong product = weak offer
\[Visual Cue\] Catalog scrolling past apparel, tumblers, jewelry, engraved gifts, and accessories

\[Tension\]
\[Spoken\] Open Completeful's Product Catalog and filter by items that make sense for your niche and buyer behavior. For occupations, some of the easiest beginner wins are mugs, tumblers, shirts, tote bags, cutting boards for culinary niches, and engraved gifts for milestone or appreciation purchases.
\[Screen Recording\] Inside Product Catalog, filters are used to browse drinkware, apparel, engraved items, and giftable products
\[Text Overlay\] Step 2: Match product to use case

\[Spoken\] Ask whether the product fits daily use, workplace pride, or gifting. A tumbler works well for nurses, teachers, and delivery drivers because it fits into a daily routine. An engraved necklace or ring may work better for a sentimental "thank you" or graduation gift.
\[Screen Recording\] Side-by-side product selection: tumbler for nurse niche, engraved jewelry for teacher appreciation niche
\[Text Overlay\] Daily use • identity • gifting

\[Spoken\] This is where Completeful's range helps because you're not locked into one format. You can test across apparel, drinkware, engraved pieces, wood items, and more, all fulfilled from the same US-based operation in Lafayette, Louisiana.
\[B-roll\] In-house production clips: tumblers being UV printed, jewelry engraved, shirts being decorated, wood products processed
\[Text Overlay\] 500+ customizable products

\[Visual Cue\] Hard cut to order dashboard and product thumbnails rapidly stacking on screen

\[Spoken\] For beginners, I recommend choosing one hero product and one supporting product. For example, a dental hygienist tumbler as the hero product and a matching tee as the supporting product. That keeps your store focused while still giving shoppers options.
\[Screen Recording\] Product shortlist board with "Hero Product" and "Supporting Product" labels
\[Text Overlay\] Start with 2 products, not 10

\[Payoff\]
\[Spoken\] By the end of this step, you know exactly what you're designing for and why. That clarity keeps your artwork more intentional and easier to scale later.
\[Text Overlay\] Product clarity improves design quality

\[Transition\]
\[Spoken\] Once your product is chosen, you can start building the design in a way that feels specific, clean, and easy to read.

**\[Chapter 3: 4:20 Building the design inside Completeful\]**

\[Setup\]
\[Spoken\] This is where many new sellers overcomplicate things. They add too many graphics, too much text, or styles that don't match the occupation. The goal is connection, not clutter.
\[Text Overlay\] Clear beats crowded
\[Visual Cue\] Before-and-after comparison of a cluttered design and a clean occupation design

\[Tension\]
\[Spoken\] Inside Completeful's Designer Tool, start by selecting your product and placing your main phrase first. Your wording should speak either to the person in the occupation or to the buyer shopping for them.
\[Screen Recording\] Product opened in Designer Tool, text field added to tumbler design area
\[Text Overlay\] Step 3: Start with the phrase

\[Spoken\] For example, instead of saying "Cool Dental Hygienist," use something more personal, playful, or proud, like "Tiny Mirrors. Big Impact." or "Dental Hygienist: Bright Smiles Daily."
\[Screen Recording\] Different text phrase options typed into the design area
\[Text Overlay\] Make it personal or giftable

\[Spoken\] Next, choose fonts that match the tone. Clean sans-serif fonts work well for professional pride. Script fonts can add warmth for gifts. Bold fonts fit humor or high-visibility drinkware and apparel.
\[Screen Recording\] Font dropdown used to compare three font styles on the same design
\[Text Overlay\] Font should match tone

\[Pattern Interrupt\]
\[Visual Cue\] Fast swap between three font options with a snap transition

\[Spoken\] Then add a simple supporting graphic, if needed, that reinforces the occupation without overcrowding the design. A small scalpel icon, a stethoscope, a tooth, or a graduation cap can add character without competing with the phrase.

Payoff: Once your wording and visual elements are working together, your design has a personality instead of just a label.

Transition: With the design structure in place, the next step is making sure it looks right on the actual product.

**\[Chapter 4: 6:55 Creating mockups that sell the idea\]**

Setup: This is where your design stops being an idea and starts becoming a product someone could actually buy and give. Mockups do most of the trust-building for you on Etsy, Shopify, and TikTok Shop.

Tension: Use Completeful's Mockup Generator to create product images from your customized design. \[Screen Recording\] Mockup Generator opens with tumbler selected \[Text Overlay\] Step 4: Generate occupation mockups

Choose angles that show the text clearly and the product at a recognizable size. For tumblers, front and angled shots work well. For tees, a direct-front flat lay or lifestyle shot works best. For gifts, choose an image that feels personal and giftable rather than just technical.

\[Spoken\] When you have multiple occupation products, keep your mockup style consistent across the collection. That helps your store look intentional instead of like a random grab of products.

Payoff: By the end of this step, your design is visible, your product is real, and your mockup is ready to compete in a busy marketplace feed.

**\[Chapter 5: 8:50 Reviewing the product before publishing\]**

Setup: Most sellers rush this step, but a quick pre-publish review can catch a lot of problems before they become customer service issues.

Tension: Before you push your product live, run a three-question check. First, does the design still read clearly at thumbnail size? Second, do the product details like title, variants, and description match the audience? Third, is the personalization or customization setup correct if you're offering a name or message?

\[Screen Recording\] Product detail page review across title, design, variants
\[Pattern Interrupt\] Three-question checklist appears full screen
\[Text Overlay\] Readable? Accurate? Personalization ready?

Payoff: A reviewed product launches cleaner and generates fewer refunds, questions, or customer disappointments.

## BONUS

\[Spoken\] Earlier I mentioned I'd share the easiest way to turn one design idea into multiple products without starting over. Here it is.

\[Spoken\] Build what I call a design core. Your core is the main phrase, the font pairing, and one supporting icon. Once that core works on one hero product, reuse it across other items with only small adjustments.
\[Text Overlay\] Bonus: Build a design core
\[Visual Cue\] One tumbler design duplicated into shirt, mug, tote, and engraved gift concepts

\[Spoken\] For example, if your dental hygienist tumbler uses the phrase "Bright Smiles Daily," a clean bold font, and a small tooth icon, you can adapt that same design to a tee by widening the layout, to a mug by centering it, or to a tote by enlarging the icon slightly.
\[Screen Recording\] Duplicate design workflow across multiple products in Completeful's design environment
\[Text Overlay\] Reuse the concept, adjust the layout

\[Spoken\] You can also create three variation types from one occupation niche: a pride version, a funny version, and a gift version. Pride says what they do well. Funny uses insider humor. Gift version adds appreciation language like "for the one who keeps every smile bright."
\[Screen Recording\] Three product variations labeled Pride, Funny, Gift
\[Text Overlay\] Pride • Funny • Gift

\[Spoken\] This approach saves time, keeps your collection consistent, and helps you test what style buyers respond to most. Instead of reinventing every product, you're building a system.
\[Text Overlay\] Build systems, not one-offs
\[Visual Cue\] Collection page fills with coordinated occupation-themed products
